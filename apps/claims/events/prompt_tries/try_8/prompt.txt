Keep a happening only when the text names it enough that a stranger could tell which one it is: a named place, a named person, a named organization and what it did, or a named paper whose publication or retraction is the news. A school, daycare, or university outbreak counts when the text names the school or the town and reports cases, quarantine, or a health-department notice. The speaker's own school rule does not.

Do not drop a happening because the chunk is short, the post is mostly commentary, or the only text is a headline. One sentence that names the act is enough, including a clause inside a rant or a paragraph about someone else. Keep a licence or approval withdrawn, a named paper retracted, a public death or hospitalization in a named place, and a dated change ("a second dose was added in 1989"). The patients may be unnamed: "two girls died in hospital in Texas after measles infections" is that event. A completed act is an event when the chunk names who did it: "withdrew the licence," "halted the vaccination drive," "defunded Gavi," "got his licence taken away," or "the journal retracted it" when the paper or its author is named. An agency now requiring new studies is an event even when the same comment is mostly accusations and schedules that were already in place: emit the requirement, not the accusation, and not the schedule. A headline that is the whole chunk is the event, including an official telling people to vaccinate when that announcement is the headline ("tells US families to vaccinate children against measles"). A named place of a wave is an outbreak, including a large country: "the measles outbreak in Canada" and "during this high-risk period" in South Carolina.

Return empty (omit; do not use other) for:

A health department or epidemiologist telling people to be up to date, check their records, or get a dose they should already have, while reporting a case, is a reminder. Not an event. A launch, shortage, halt, quarantine order, or new mandate is an event.

"About to," "they will," "while they cut themselves off from," or any other speculative aside is not an event. A comma-separated history of past wrongs is empty. One item in that list is an event only when that item itself names who decided what and that it happened.

event_type (exactly one per event):
- outbreak: infection type. Either the start of a shift from low/steady transmission to ongoing higher transmission in a named place, or a later update on that same wave (new case counts, clinic warnings, hospitalizations). Use outbreak when the text reports community or multi-site spread, a rising or accumulated case load, or hospitalizations from that wave — even if it says "cases," "cluster," or "detected," and even if no one uses the word outbreak. Put counts in case_count. Disease infections only, not vaccine reactions. A wastewater detection or a clinician's private caseload is not an outbreak.
- notable_case: infection type. A public story of an isolated infection that is itself noteworthy: a named or public figure, a news or official report of a death or rare complication, first-in-a-decade, an imported case with a story, a specific exposure site (airport, hospital, funeral, named school), or a health department confirming one case or warning that people at a named event were exposed. Those are notable_case, not cases and not institutional_action. There must be something noteworthy besides the fact of cases in a wave (an update on a wave is outbreak). A case the text says is not linked to the named outbreaks is not an event. The named counties in that same sentence are still outbreaks, one event each. A disease-death in a wave can still be a second notable_case if it is a public/official case. Not the speaker's family, coworker, or someone they know. Not vaccine-attributed injury or death.
- cases: infection type. Leftover isolated infections of the disease that are neither a wave nor noteworthy — a few infections in one place with no community-spread signal and nothing special about who or where. Not vaccine reactions. "Measles reported in Wisconsin" or "cases in Oklahoma," with no wording that this is separate from a wave, is outbreak. cases is only a count the text sets apart from a wave ("two cases, hasn't had an outbreak," one case in a city the text does not call an outbreak). If unsure between outbreak and cases, use outbreak. If unsure between notable_case and cases, use cases. "Someone had a mild case" with no name and no place is not cases. An unnamed toddler who is one of several cases in a wave is not a separate event. Several named exposure sites are one notable_case each, not one event for the whole list.
- vaccine_injury: a specific public case of harm attributed to a vaccine (injury or death) — a named person, a news story about a particular patient or family, a court / VICP docket, or a published case report of a specific patient. Not a known side-effect class with no case attached (myocarditis, blood clots, "Dengvaxia risk"). Not the speaker's own shot. Not generic "vaccines cause autism." A ruling, a denied claim, or a lawsuit with many claimants is institutional_action, not vaccine_injury.
- institutional_action: a government, regulator, court, or named organization did something — mandate or exemption, schedule change, license / approval / recall, firing or appointment, court ruling, official data release, gaining or losing elimination status (including "measles-free" status). A shortage, a resignation or firing, and an award are institutional_action, not other. Not a person asking questions or filing a request. Not a description of rules that already exist. Not voting, and keeping the current recommendation, is one event. One vaccine's votes in the same meeting are one event, not one event per motion.
- study_publication: the text reports that a named paper, review, or retraction landed. Not a link offered as evidence. Set study_action to publication or retraction. Publication and retraction of the same paper are two events when the text reports both.
- other: last resort for a happening that is none of the above. Not for journalism, FOIA, patents, campaigns, routine availability, a shortage, a resignation, an award, or a free vaccine clinic in a named city (that clinic is institutional_action).

If the post is about Florida ending school mandates and also notes Massachusetts keeping them and a CA/OR/WA alliance, emit all three as institutional_action (still at most 4 events total). Mark Florida focal and the others mentioned.

Place (one value per slot, English short name, or empty):

place_scope (exactly one):
- at_named_place: the finest filled place is where the text locates the happening. A country with no city named is this when the text puts the wave there ("a large outbreak in Morocco," "an outbreak in Samoa," "the measles outbreak in Canada"). A wave is at_named_place, not countrywide.
- countrywide: the claim is about the country as a whole, and the country slot is filled. Elimination status, a national tally, "worst outbreak in decades in the United States" with no state, or a national act whose only place is the country: withdrawing a licence, halting or banning a vaccine, striking a doctor off a national register. This does not attach to a state or a city. Do not use countrywide for a wave.
- unplaced: the happening is somewhere and the text never names the place. A vote whose text never names a country is unplaced. Do not fill United States because the actor is ACIP, the FDA, or the CDC. Do not set countrywide when the country slot is empty.
- global: the claim has no place. A worldwide eradication, or a declared global return, that you are keeping. Place slots empty. A bare "disease is back" is empty, not global.
