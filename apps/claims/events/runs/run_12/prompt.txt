Keep a happening only when the text names it enough that a stranger could tell which one it is: a named place, a named person, a named organization and what it did, or a named paper whose publication or retraction is the news. A school, daycare, or university outbreak counts when the text names the school or the town and reports cases, quarantine, or a health-department notice. The speaker's own school rule does not.

Return empty (omit; do not use other) for:
- The speaker's own shots, boosters, titers, side effects, or childhood illness
- The speaker's or their family's illness or death, or an unnamed person the speaker knows who got sick or died
- "My university / my school / my daycare required vaccination," or a sign the speaker noticed. A named school changing a rule, or a health department closing a named school, is an event.
- Unnamed or personal vaccine-reaction anecdotes (vaccine_injury is only public stories)
- Known vaccine side-effect classes with no specific public case (myocarditis, clots, a named product's "increased risk")
- A list of which vaccines are mandatory, recommended, or still on the schedule, with no change. A reported change, repeal, or new dose is an event.
- Routine operations: a pharmacy stocking a vaccine, a clinic's normal reminders, a program that has already been running. A launch, shortage, halt, quarantine order, or new mandate is an event.
- A vote, meeting, or order whose outcome the text does not give ("voting on" a vaccine, "will vote," "it's not clear what they will decide"). A vote that states what passed or failed is an event.
- "It," "they," "the vaccine," or "that year" when the chunk never says what those words refer to. A named state is not enough: "the year Wisconsin decided to mandate the vaccine" is empty when the year and the product are never named.
- A vague accusation with no reported decision ("got it wrong again at someone's behest," an agency "burned the documents" or "admitted lying," with no date and no source)
- "Disease is back," a national case total, a CDC warning of an uptick across a country, or "cases hit a multi-year high" with no named place that is the wave. That is not an outbreak and not an institutional_action. A named state, city, school, or a small country that is itself the place of the wave is an outbreak. Losing elimination status is one institutional_action; do not also emit an outbreak because the wave lasted a year.
- "My area," "my state," "where I live," "nationwide," "across the country," "multiple places," when no specific place is named. "Cases in countries like Mexico, the United States, and Canada" is not one outbreak and not one event per country. An increase in cases "here" is not an outbreak.
- A lab claim that vials contain a chemical. Not an event.
- A program that "is running again" or "vaccinates kids today," with no date for the restart. The earlier halt can still be an event. Do not emit a vaccine clinic because the text says one was set up or was empty. "Got a bit firmer" about a long-standing recommendation is not a new act. "An increase in TB cases here" is not an outbreak. A PSA that says outbreaks are popping up across the country is not an outbreak. A title that says an agency will investigate is not an event when the body never reports that it started.
- A citation used as proof. study_publication is only when the text reports that a named paper or review was published or retracted as the happening.
- Generic safety/dosing/biology, hypotheticals, jokes, coverage percentages
- A causal rant with no happening ("anti-vax caused disease to come back"). A list of several diseases in one rant is not several events.
- Journalism, investigations, Senate testimony, publicity or literacy campaigns, deleted social-media posts
- FOIA / records requests and talking-point FOI results. Keep only if a court orders disclosure or an agency formally releases or withholds.
- Private patent applications. Keep only if a patent office grants, rejects, or invalidates.
- Historical setup and bibliography: old global death stats, every disease the speaker ever had. A dated public happening the text actually reports (a named outbreak in a named year) is an event, including when the post is about emails or testimony and the headline names that outbreak. "I was living in Texas last year when we had that huge outbreak" is that outbreak. The speaker's titer is not. Do not copy posted_at onto it.

event_type (exactly one per event):
- outbreak: infection type. Either the start of a shift from low/steady transmission to ongoing higher transmission in a named place, or a later update on that same wave (new case counts, clinic warnings, hospitalizations). Use outbreak when the text reports community or multi-site spread, a rising or accumulated case load, or hospitalizations from that wave — even if it says "cases," "cluster," or "detected," and even if no one uses the word outbreak. Put counts in case_count. Disease infections only, not vaccine reactions. A wastewater detection or a clinician's private caseload is not an outbreak.
- notable_case: infection type. A public story of an isolated infection that is itself noteworthy: a named or public figure, a news or official report of a death or rare complication, first-in-a-decade, an imported case with a story, a specific exposure site (airport, hospital, funeral, named school), or a health department confirming one case or warning that people at a named event were exposed. Those are notable_case, not cases and not institutional_action. There must be something noteworthy besides the fact of cases in a wave (an update on a wave is outbreak). A case the text says is not linked to the named outbreaks is not an event. The named counties in that same sentence are still outbreaks, one event each. A disease-death in a wave can still be a second notable_case if it is a public/official case. Not the speaker's family, coworker, or someone they know. Not vaccine-attributed injury or death.
- cases: infection type. Leftover isolated infections of the disease that are neither a wave nor noteworthy — a few infections in one place with no community-spread signal and nothing special about who or where. Not vaccine reactions. If unsure between outbreak and cases, use outbreak. If unsure between notable_case and cases, use cases. "Someone had a mild case" with no name and no place is not cases. An unnamed toddler who is one of several cases in a wave is not a separate event. Several named exposure sites are one notable_case each, not one event for the whole list.
- vaccine_injury: a specific public case of harm attributed to a vaccine (injury or death) — a named person, a news story about a particular patient or family, a court / VICP docket, or a published case report of a specific patient. Not a known side-effect class with no case attached (myocarditis, blood clots, "Dengvaxia risk"). Not the speaker's own shot. Not generic "vaccines cause autism."
- institutional_action: a government, regulator, court, or named organization did something — mandate or exemption, schedule change, license / approval / recall, firing or appointment, court ruling, official data release, gaining or losing elimination status. Not a person asking questions or filing a request. Not a description of rules that already exist. Not voting, and keeping the current recommendation, is one event. One vaccine's votes in the same meeting are one event, not one event per motion.
- study_publication: the text reports that a named paper, review, or retraction landed. Not a link offered as evidence.
- other: last resort for a happening that is none of the above. Not for journalism, FOIA, patents, campaigns, routine availability, or a free vaccine clinic in a named city (that clinic is institutional_action).

If the post is about Florida ending school mandates and also notes Massachusetts keeping them and a CA/OR/WA alliance, emit all three as institutional_action (still at most 4 events total). Mark Florida focal and the others mentioned.

A second country goes in admin1_other only if it is a second place of the same happening; otherwise it is a second event or nothing.
